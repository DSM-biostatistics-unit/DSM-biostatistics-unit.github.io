## Research


My research focuses on two main areas: medical statistics and public health. In medical statistics, my primary interests lie in clinical prediction models, particularly their calibration, clinical utility, and associated uncertainty, including uncertainty in individual-level clinical decision-making. My work in this area has a strong focus on applications in fetal medicine, including an ongoing collaboration with Imperial College London on risk prediction for small-for-gestational-age births, fetal growth restriction, and preeclampsia (TRUFFLE-2, and the upcoming TRUFFLE-3 and TRUFFLE-4). In public health, I am a Lead Collaborator of the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) Study, contributing to research on population health and disease burden. My interests include maternal, child, and adolescent health, as well as methodological approaches to the redistribution of ill-defined and unspecified causes of death. I am currently leading a project within a European Joint Action on Diabetes and Cardiovascular Diseases, focused on developing and applying methods to redistribute deaths attributed to unspecified diabetes between type 1 and type 2 diabetes. The project aims to improve the accuracy of cause-specific mortality estimates and strengthen the evidence base for epidemiological research and public health decision-making.
