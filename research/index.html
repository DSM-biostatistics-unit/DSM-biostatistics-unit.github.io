Research

Our methodological work bridges classical statistical frameworks and modern predictive modeling, driven by a shared commitment to causal inference, interpretability, and translational impact. Across our research, we work closely with clinicians, translating methodological advances into tools that can genuinely support clinical decision-making.

This commitment takes shape along two complementary lines of research. The first centers on multi-state models for complex time-to-event data and on methods for extracting actionable insights from electronic health records (EHR), paired with a growing interest in machine learning and deep learning for prediction and decision-making; it also extends to collaborations with pharmaceutical companies to generate real-world evidence through comparative EHR studies and target trial emulation. The second develops statistical and machine learning methods for high-dimensional data in pursuit of interpretable, well-calibrated prognostic and diagnostic models, spanning genetic association studies, polygenic risk scores, rare-variant analyses, clustering techniques, and deep learning for time-series and signal data such as EHR and ECG, with the aim of turning these methods into patient stratification and risk-prediction tools for precision medicine.

Alongside this research, we provide ongoing biostatistical consulting to clinical researchers across our department, supporting the design and analysis of their studies.
